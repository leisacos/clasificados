Buenos Aires' Congress at golden hour, seen from where almost nobody sees it. The **Palacio del Congreso** opened in 1906, and its dome rises about **80 metres** over the city. From the street it looks huge. From up high, it's a different story.

If you're searching for how to visit the Buenos Aires Congress building, here's what you need: where it is, how to get there, whether you can go inside and the best moments to photograph it.

# Quick facts about the Buenos Aires Congress

- **What it is:** the seat of Argentina's national legislature, the Palacio del Congreso Nacional.
- **Opened:** 1906.
- **Dome:** about 80 metres high, the landmark you can spot from several blocks away.
- **Where:** Plaza del Congreso, at the western end of Avenida de Mayo, in the Monserrat and Balvanera area.
- **Cost to see it from outside:** free, since it faces a public square.

# How to get to the Congreso

The easiest way is the **Subte**. Line A has a **Congreso** station right by the plaza. Many bus lines run through the area, so Google Maps or the BA Cómo Llego app will show you the best route from your hotel. Check current schedules and fares before you go, as they change often.

You can also walk. **Avenida de Mayo** runs straight from the Casa Rosada and Plaza de Mayo to the Congreso, roughly 1.5 km, past historic cafés and buildings. It's a great way to see the old centre.

# Can you go inside?

Yes, in many cases. The Congress has historically offered **guided visits** that show the chambers and the halls, and these have usually been free. Times, languages, ID requirements and booking rules change, and visits can be suspended on days when the chambers are in session, so **check the official Congress website before you go** and book if it asks you to.

# When to go and how to photograph it

- **Golden hour:** the light on the stone and the dome is at its best in the last hour before sunset.
- **From the plaza:** walk across the Plaza del Congreso so the whole building and the monument in front of it fit in one frame.
- **From above:** the video shows the building from high up. If you plan to fly a drone, check Argentina's current drone rules and the restrictions around government buildings first. They can be strict.
- **Stay aware:** the area is busy by day and sometimes hosts demonstrations, so keep an eye on the news and watch your belongings, as in any big city.
