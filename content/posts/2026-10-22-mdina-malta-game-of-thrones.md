You've seen this city in **Game of Thrones**. **Mdina**, Malta's "Silent City", stood in for King's Landing in season 1. It has around 4,000 years of history, and only a few hundred people still live inside its walls. Did you recognise it?

# Mdina Malta Game of Thrones: quick facts

- **What it is:** a walled hilltop town in the middle of the island of Malta, nicknamed the **Silent City** because it is so quiet inside the walls.
- **Game of Thrones:** the town appears as King's Landing in the first season. Check a current filming-locations list if you want to match specific scenes to specific streets.
- **History:** roughly 4,000 years of settlement, and Malta's old capital before the Knights moved the centre of power to the harbour area and founded Valletta.
- **Cost:** wandering the streets is generally free. Museums, the cathedral and some viewpoints may charge, so check current prices and opening hours.
- **Time needed:** half a day is enough for Mdina and its neighbour Rabat, or a full day if you add the cliffs.

# How to get to Mdina from Valletta and the airport

Malta is small, and Mdina is in the middle of it, so getting there is easy from almost anywhere.

- **Bus:** public buses connect Valletta and the main resorts with the Mdina and Rabat area. Routes and numbers change, so check the current timetable before you go.
- **Taxi or rideshare:** the quickest option from the airport. Agree the fare or use an app, and ask to be dropped near the main gate.
- **Rental car:** useful for the rest of the island, but cars are restricted inside Mdina. Park outside the walls and walk in.

# What to see in Mdina, the Silent City

- **Mdina Gate:** you cross a bridge over the dry moat and walk through the main gate. This is the entrance fans of the show look for.
- **The narrow lanes:** the street plan is a maze of honey-coloured limestone, with small squares and old palaces. Get a little lost on purpose.
- **St Paul's Cathedral:** the town's main church, with a busy square in front of it.
- **The bastions:** from the walls you look out over much of the island on a clear day.
- **A café stop:** a slow coffee on a terrace is the classic way to enjoy a town this quiet.

# When to go to Mdina

Mdina is pleasant most of the year. Summer is hot and the stone holds the heat, so start early. Spring and autumn are generally milder for walking. Evenings tend to be calmer once the day-trippers leave, which fits the Silent City nickname. Weather and crowds vary, so check forecasts for your dates.

# What else to do nearby

- **Rabat:** the town right next to Mdina, with churches and early Christian sites. Check what is open.
- **Valletta:** the capital, a short trip away. It is a good partner for a second day.
- **The coast:** Malta has dramatic cliffs and sea views, handy if you have a car or a tour.
