Sunday sunset in Buenos Aires: palm trees, boats on the lake and the whole skyline turning pink. That's the **Bosques de Palermo**, the big green lung of the city and one of the best free things to do in Buenos Aires when you want a break from the traffic.

# Bosques de Palermo at a glance

- **What it is:** a huge public park, officially part of the **Parque Tres de Febrero**, with lakes, tree-lined paths, gardens and lawns.
- **Where:** the Palermo neighbourhood, in the north of the city, along Avenida del Libertador.
- **Cost:** entry to the park is free. Some attractions inside, such as boat rentals or the Japanese Garden, charge separately, so check prices before you go.
- **Best time:** the hour before sunset, especially at the weekend, when the light goes pink over the water.

# How to get to the Bosques de Palermo

The park is large, so pick the entrance that suits you.

- **Subte (metro):** Line D has stations along the edge of Palermo, including **Plaza Italia** and **Palermo**. From there it's a short walk into the park. Check the current line and station details before you travel.
- **Bus, taxi or rideshare:** from Palermo Soho, Recoleta or Belgrano it's a short ride. Tell the driver the landmark you want (the Rosedal or the Lago de Regatas) rather than just "Bosques".
- **On foot or by bike:** if you're staying in Palermo, walking is the easiest way. The paths are good for running and cycling too.

# What to do in the Bosques de Palermo

- **Walk around the lakes.** The water, the palms and the weeping trees make this one of the prettiest corners of the city. Boats on the lake are part of the scene in the sunset video.
- **See the Rosedal.** The rose garden is the park's best-known spot and looks best in spring and early summer. Rose bloom depends on the season, so don't expect the same display all year.
- **Picnic on the grass.** Locals bring a mate, a blanket and something to share. It's a very porteño way to spend a Sunday afternoon.
- **Run, cycle or skate.** The loops around the park are popular with joggers and cyclists, particularly early in the morning and at the end of the day.
- **Visit the nearby sights.** Depending on the day, you can link the park with the Japanese Garden, the Planetarium or nearby museums. Opening hours and prices change, so check each one before you go.

# Photo tips for sunset

- Arrive **45 to 60 minutes before sunset** to find a spot by the water.
- Frame the **palm trees and the boats** against the sky. The skyline behind the lake is what makes the shot.
- Stay ten minutes after the sun goes down; the colours often get better, not worse.
- Weekends are busier, so go on a weekday if you want fewer people in your photos.

# Safety and practical tips

Like any big city park, it's best to stay in the busier areas and leave before it gets properly dark, unless you're with other people. Keep your phone and camera close, and use the same common-sense habits you would in any big city. Bring water and sun protection in summer, and a light jacket for the evening.

# Where to stay near the Bosques de Palermo

The best base is **Palermo** itself: leafy streets, plenty of cafés and restaurants, and the park within walking distance. If you want a more classic feel, **Recoleta** is close by. # Frequently asked questions

## Are the Bosques de Palermo free?
Yes, entering the park is free. Some things inside, like boat rentals or the Japanese Garden, have their own prices and hours, so check before you go.

## What is the best time to visit the Bosques de Palermo?
The hour before sunset is the prettiest, when the light turns pink over the lake. Spring is also a lovely season for the gardens and the Rosedal.

## How do I get to the Bosques de Palermo?
The easiest way is by Subte Line D to Plaza Italia or Palermo and then a short walk, or a taxi or rideshare from anywhere in Palermo.

## Is it safe to go to the Bosques de Palermo at sunset?
Many people go for sunset, especially at the weekend. Stay in the busier areas, keep your belongings close and leave before it gets very dark if you're on your own.

# Qué hacer en los Bosques de Palermo

- **Caminá alrededor de los lagos.** El agua, las palmeras y los sauces hacen de este uno de los rincones más lindos de la ciudad. Los botes en el lago son parte de la escena del video del atardecer.
- **Visitá el Rosedal.** El jardín de rosas es el lugar más conocido del parque y se ve mejor en primavera y principios del verano. La floración depende de la época, así que no esperes el mismo espectáculo todo el año.
- **Hacé un picnic en el pasto.** Los locales llevan el mate, una manta y algo para compartir. Es una forma muy porteña de pasar un domingo a la tarde.
- **Corré, andá en bici o en patines.** Los circuitos del parque son muy usados por quienes salen a correr o pedalear, sobre todo temprano a la mañana y al final del día.
- **Aprovechá lo que hay cerca.** Según el día, podés combinar el parque con el Jardín Japonés, el Planetario o museos cercanos. Los horarios y precios cambian, así que verificalos antes de ir.

# Tips para fotografiar el atardecer

- Llegá **45 a 60 minutos antes** de la puesta del sol para conseguir un buen lugar junto al agua.
- Encuadrá las **palmeras y los botes** contra el cielo. La ciudad de fondo detrás del lago es lo que hace la foto.
- Quedate diez minutos después de que se ponga el sol: los colores suelen mejorar.
- Los fines de semana hay más gente; si querés fotos más despejadas, andá un día de semana.

# Seguridad y consejos prácticos

Como en cualquier parque grande de la ciudad, conviene quedarse en las zonas con más gente y irse antes de que oscurezca del todo, salvo que vayas acompañado. Tené el celular y la cámara a mano y usá el mismo sentido común que en cualquier ciudad grande. Llevá agua y protector solar en verano, y una campera liviana para la noche.

# Dónde alojarse cerca de los Bosques de Palermo
