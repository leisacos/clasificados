Locals say these rocks were once trolls. Off **Reynisfjara black sand beach** in Iceland, the sea stacks of **Reynisdrangar** rise out of the water, and the legend says two trolls were dragging a ship ashore when dawn caught them and turned them to stone. Would you visit at dawn?

# Reynisfjara at a glance

- **What it is:** a black volcanic sand beach on Iceland's South Coast, with the **Reynisdrangar** sea stacks just offshore.
- **Where:** near the village of **Vík í Mýrdal**, on the Ring Road.
- **Cost:** visiting the beach is generally free. Parking arrangements can change, so check before you go.
- **Biggest warning:** Reynisfjara is famous for **sneaker waves**. Stay well back from the water.

# The legend of the Reynisdrangar trolls

The folk tale goes like this: two trolls were hauling a three-masted ship toward land at night. Trolls can't be out in daylight, and dawn caught them before they finished, so they froze into the tall stone pillars you see in the water. It's one of the best-known stories of Icelandic folklore, and it's the hook of my reel. The stacks are real rock, of course, and the way they stand in the surf makes it easy to see why people told the story.

# How to get to Reynisfjara

- **By car:** it's roughly a 2.5-hour drive east of Reykjavík along the Ring Road (Route 1), so most people see it on a South Coast day trip or on a longer road trip. Driving times depend on weather and season.
- **By tour:** many South Coast tours stop here, usually along with waterfalls. This is the easy option if you don't want to drive in winter conditions.
- **Public transport:** options are limited and seasonal, so check current timetables.

# Staying safe: sneaker waves

Reynisfjara is beautiful, and it's also dangerous. Waves here can run much farther up the beach than the ones before them, with no warning, and the water is very cold. Visitors have been hurt and even killed.

- **Stay well back from the water**, and never turn your back on the sea.
- Keep off the lower rocks and don't go out on them for a photo.
- **Watch the warning signs** and any warning lights at the beach, and follow the current guidance.
- Keep children close, and think twice about the beach on stormy days.

# When to go

- **Dawn or sunset** is the mood in my reel, with soft light on the stacks and fewer people. Sunrise and sunset times in Iceland change a lot through the year (very late sunrises in winter, almost no night in summer), so check the times for your dates.
- **Summer** has long days and easier driving, but the car park fills up by midday.
- **Winter** is dramatic and quiet, with short daylight. Roads can close, so check road conditions before you set off.

# What else to see nearby

- **Dyrhólaey**, a nearby headland with views along the coast.

# Cómo visitarla con seguridad: las olas traicioneras

Reynisfjara es hermosa, pero también peligrosa. Acá las olas pueden llegar mucho más lejos que las anteriores, sin aviso, y el agua está helada. Hubo visitantes lastimados e incluso muertos.

- **Mantenete bien lejos del agua** y nunca le des la espalda al mar.
- No te subas a las rocas bajas ni salgas a ellas para una foto.
- **Mirá los carteles** y las luces de advertencia en la playa, y seguí las indicaciones vigentes.
- Tené cerca a los chicos y pensalo dos veces los días de tormenta.

# Cuándo ir

- **Amanecer o atardecer** es el clima de mi reel, con luz suave sobre los islotes y menos gente. Los horarios del sol en Islandia cambian muchísimo durante el año (amaneceres muy tardíos en invierno, casi sin noche en verano), así que chequeá las horas para tus fechas.
- **Verano** tiene días largos y es más fácil manejar, pero el estacionamiento se llena al mediodía.
- **Invierno** es dramático y tranquilo, con poca luz de día. Las rutas pueden cerrarse, así que mirá el estado de los caminos antes de salir.
