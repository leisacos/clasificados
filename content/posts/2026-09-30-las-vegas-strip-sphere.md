From the lights of the **Strip** to the incredible visuals of the **Sphere**, Vegas is one of those places that feels almost unreal. And it's not all casinos: the city is packed with public art and installations that turn your world upside down, sometimes literally 😁

If Vegas is on your bucket list, this is your sign to start planning ✈️ Here's how to approach a first trip to Las Vegas beyond the casino floor.

# Las Vegas at a glance

- **What it is:** a desert city built around one long avenue, the Las Vegas Strip, lined with giant resorts, shows and restaurants.
- **How long to stay:** 3 to 4 days is a comfortable first visit, and a few more if you want to fit in a national park or the Grand Canyon.
- **Best seasons:** spring and autumn are usually the most comfortable. Summer days can be extremely hot, so plan outdoor things for early morning or evening. Check the forecast before you go.
- **Getting around:** the Strip is longer than it looks. Distances between resorts add up, so wear comfortable shoes and use the monorail, rideshares or taxis for the longer hops. Check current routes and prices before you go.

# The Sphere

The giant LED globe lights up the skyline every night, and you can see the outside for free from the Strip. For the full experience, book a **show or immersive film inside**. Tickets sell out, so book ahead, and check the venue's official site for current programming and prices.

Photo tip: the exterior changes constantly, so give it a few minutes and shoot from a bit of distance to fit the whole globe in the frame. A wide lens or your phone's ultra-wide mode helps.

# Public art and free things to do on the Strip

You don't need to gamble to fill a day in Vegas. Many of the best-known sights cost nothing, though opening times and shows change, so check before you go:

- **Walk the Strip at night.** The lights are the main show, and every resort looks completely different after dark.
- **Wander the resort lobbies.** Several have free attractions, gardens or large installations that change with the season.
- **Hunt for public art.** Vegas has murals and sculpture, including pieces in the Arts District and Downtown. Look for them while you walk.
- **See Fremont Street.** The covered pedestrian street in Downtown has a light canopy and street performers, and it's a good contrast to the modern Strip.

If you love places that turn light into spectacle, you might also enjoy [Arbórea Magna, Buenos Aires' tree of light](/posts/arborea-magna-buenos-aires/), or the nightly light show at the Supertrees in my [Marina Bay Sands and Singapore guide](/posts/marina-bay-sands-singapore-guide/).

# Getting to Las Vegas

**Harry Reid International Airport (LAS)** is only a 10–15 minute ride from the Strip, which makes arrival easy. From there you can take a taxi, rideshare or airport shuttle, and the main resorts are all close.

# Best day trips from Las Vegas

- **Grand Canyon**: by road, small plane or helicopter. It's a long day by road, so many people choose a guided tour or an overnight stay.
- **Hoover Dam**: under an hour away, and often combined with the Grand Canyon.
- **Red Rock Canyon**: desert hikes and scenic drives just outside the city, good for a half day. A timed-entry reservation can be required in busy periods, so check before you go.
- **Valley of Fire**: red sandstone that glows at sunset, about an hour from the city.

Bring plenty of water for any desert outing, and start early in warm months.

# Where to stay in Las Vegas

Mid-Strip hotels put you within walking distance of the main sights. **Downtown / Fremont Street** is cheaper and has a more old-school Vegas feel. Always check the **resort fee** when comparing prices, because it is often added on top of the room rate and can change the real cost.

# Frequently asked questions

## Is Las Vegas worth visiting if you don't gamble?
Yes. The Strip's lights, the Sphere, the shows, the food and the nearby desert parks give you plenty to do without touching a slot machine.

## Can you see the Sphere without a ticket?
You can see the exterior and its LED displays for free from the Strip. Tickets are only needed for the experiences inside, and they can sell out, so book ahead.

## How many days do you need in Las Vegas?
Three or four days cover the Strip, the Sphere and Downtown. Add a day or two if you want to do the Grand Canyon, Hoover Dam or Red Rock Canyon.

## What is the best way to get from the airport to the Strip?
The airport is only about 10–15 minutes from the Strip, so a taxi, rideshare or shuttle are all quick. Check current prices and pickup points when you land.

# Arte público y cosas gratis para hacer en el Strip

No hace falta apostar para llenar un día en Las Vegas. Muchas de las atracciones más conocidas no cuestan nada, aunque los horarios y los shows cambian, así que chequeá antes de ir:

- **Caminá el Strip de noche.** Las luces son el gran show, y cada resort se ve completamente distinto después de que oscurece.
- **Recorré los lobbies de los resorts.** Varios tienen atracciones gratuitas, jardines o grandes instalaciones que cambian con la temporada.
- **Buscá arte público.** Las Vegas tiene murales y esculturas, incluso en el Arts District y en Downtown. Fijate mientras caminás.
- **Conocé Fremont Street.** La calle peatonal techada de Downtown tiene un techo de luces y artistas callejeros, y es un buen contraste con el Strip moderno.

Si te gustan los lugares donde la luz se vuelve espectáculo, quizás también te guste [Arbórea Magna, el árbol de luz de Buenos Aires](/es/posts/arborea-magna-buenos-aires/), o el show de luces nocturno de los Supertrees en mi [guía de Marina Bay Sands y Singapur](/es/posts/marina-bay-sands-singapore-guide/).

# Las mejores excursiones desde Las Vegas

- **Gran Cañón**: en auto, avioneta o helicóptero. En auto es un día largo, así que mucha gente elige un tour guiado o pasar una noche.
- **Represa Hoover**: a menos de una hora, y suele combinarse con el Gran Cañón.
- **Red Rock Canyon**: caminatas en el desierto y rutas panorámicas a las afueras de la ciudad, ideal para medio día. En épocas de mucha demanda puede pedirse reserva con horario, así que chequeá antes de ir.
- **Valley of Fire**: arenisca roja que brilla al atardecer, a más o menos una hora de la ciudad.

Llevá bastante agua para cualquier salida por el desierto y arrancá temprano en los meses de calor.

# Dónde alojarse en Las Vegas

Los hoteles del centro del Strip te dejan a distancia caminable de lo principal. **Downtown / Fremont Street** es más barato y tiene un aire más de la Las Vegas clásica. Siempre revisá el **resort fee** al comparar precios, porque suele sumarse al valor de la habitación y puede cambiar el costo real.

# Preguntas frecuentes

## ¿Vale la pena ir a Las Vegas si no apostás?
Sí. Las luces del Strip, la Sphere, los shows, la comida y los parques del desierto cercanos te dan muchísimo para hacer sin tocar una tragamonedas.

## ¿Se puede ver la Sphere sin entrada?
El exterior y sus pantallas LED se ven gratis desde el Strip. Las entradas solo hacen falta para las experiencias de adentro, y pueden agotarse, así que reservá con tiempo.

## ¿Cuántos días hacen falta en Las Vegas?
Tres o cuatro días cubren el Strip, la Sphere y Downtown. Sumá uno o dos días más si querés hacer el Gran Cañón, la Represa Hoover o Red Rock Canyon.

## ¿Cuál es la mejor forma de ir del aeropuerto al Strip?
El aeropuerto queda a unos 10–15 minutos del Strip, así que taxi, app de viajes o shuttle son todos rápidos. Chequeá precios y puntos de encuentro actuales al llegar.
