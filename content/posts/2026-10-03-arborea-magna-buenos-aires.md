Red at sunset, purple after dark. **Arbórea Magna** is a giant tree of light in Buenos Aires' Parque de la Innovación, and it's one of the most photogenic free things to do in the city.

# When to go

Get there **just before sunset**. As the sky goes dark you'll watch the tree change colour, from warm reds to deep purple. It's the kind of shot that works even on a phone: frame it against the last light in the sky, then stay ten more minutes for the night colours.

# What is Arbórea Magna?

**Arbórea Magna** is a large illuminated tree sculpture in the Parque de la Innovación, in Núñez. By day it's an interesting structure; its real moment is the light show as the sky darkens. It's an open-air public space, so there's no ticket and no fixed visit length: you can stay five minutes or an hour. Light schedules and park opening hours can change, so check before you go.

# How to get there

Arbórea Magna is in the **Parque de la Innovación in Núñez**, right next to River Plate's Monumental stadium, in the north of the city. Entry is **free**.

- From Palermo or Belgrano it's a short taxi or rideshare.
- Trains on the Mitre line and several bus routes stop nearby. Check Google Maps or the BA Cómo Llego app for the best route from where you're staying.
- Avoid match days at the Monumental unless you want the full football crowd experience.
- If you take a taxi or rideshare back at night, order it from the park or a main avenue rather than a side street, as in any big city after dark.

# Photo tips for Arbórea Magna

- **Arrive 30 to 45 minutes before sunset** so you can try the tree against a bright sky and again once the colours take over.
- Use a **tripod or lean your phone on a wall** for the night shots. Low light blurs handheld photos.
- Try a **low angle** looking up through the branches, then step back for a wide shot with the sky.
- Tap the brightest part of the tree on your screen and lower the exposure a little, so the colours don't wash out to white.
- Visit on a **clear evening if you can**: clouds can mute the sunset, though they also make the purple pop.
