This waterfall was almost a power plant. **Gullfoss**, the "Golden Falls", is one of Iceland's most famous waterfalls, and in the early 1900s investors wanted to dam it for electricity. **Sigríður Tómasdóttir**, who grew up beside it, fought to stop them. Today it's protected, and you can stand at the edge and feel the spray. Would you visit?

# Gullfoss at a glance

- **What it is:** a big two-step waterfall on the **Hvítá** river, in southwest Iceland.
- **Where:** it's the best-known stop on the **Golden Circle**, along with Þingvellir and the Geysir area.
- **Cost:** seeing the falls is generally free. Parking and facilities can change, so check before you go.
- **Good to know:** the path gets wet and slippery in spray and ice. Wear a rain jacket and shoes with grip.

# The story: how Gullfoss was almost a power plant

Early in the 20th century, foreign investors showed interest in using the falls to produce electricity. The story, as it's usually told, is that **Sigríður Tómasdóttir**, a farmer's daughter who grew up near the river, spoke up against the plan and campaigned to keep the waterfall as it was. The details of the legal side are more complicated than the legend, but the outcome is clear: Gullfoss was never dammed. Today the land is protected, and there's a memorial to her near the falls. Check the details at the visitor centre or on the information signs when you go.

It's one of the reasons I like this place. The falls aren't just beautiful; they're a reminder that places like this can be lost, and that one person can make a difference.

# How to get to Gullfoss from Reykjavík

- **By car:** roughly a 1.5–2-hour drive from Reykjavík, depending on weather and which route you take. Most people combine it with Þingvellir and Geysir in one day.
- **By tour:** Golden Circle tours are among the most common day trips from Reykjavík, and they're an easy option in winter when roads can be icy.
- **Public transport:** options are limited and seasonal, so check current timetables.

# When to go

- **Summer** has very long days and the easiest driving, but it's also the busiest. Go early or late in the day for fewer people.
- **Winter** is quieter and the ice and snow make the scenery dramatic, but daylight is short and some paths near the edge can close because of ice. Check road conditions before you set off.
- **Rainbows** can form in the spray when the sun is out, which is what you can see in my drone footage.

# What to see and do at Gullfoss

- Walk the **upper path** for a view along the whole falls.
- Take the **lower path** (when it's open) to get close to the edge of the first drop. Stay behind the barriers.
- Look at the **canyon** the river has cut below the falls: from above you can see how the water turns and disappears into it.
- Stop at the **visitor centre** for a hot drink and a warm-up.

A principios del siglo XX, inversores extranjeros se interesaron en usar las cataratas para producir electricidad. - Recorré el **sendero superior** para ver toda la catarata a lo largo.
