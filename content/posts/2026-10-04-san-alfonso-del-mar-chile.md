This pool is **1 km long**. San Alfonso del Mar in **Algarrobo, Chile**, holds around **250 million litres of Pacific Ocean water**, and for years it held the record as the largest swimming pool in the world.

# The record and how it works

- San Alfonso del Mar was recognised by **Guinness World Records in 2006** as the world's largest swimming pool: just over **1 kilometre long** and covering around **8 hectares**.
- Instead of being a closed tank, it's filled with **seawater pumped from the Pacific** and filtered, so it looks like a calm, turquoise lagoon right next to the ocean.
- The technology behind it was developed in Chile and later used to build similar "crystal lagoons" in other countries.
- The record was later overtaken by bigger lagoons elsewhere, but it's still one of the most surprising sights on Chile's coast.

# Where it is

Algarrobo is a beach town on Chile's central coast, roughly a **2-hour drive from Santiago**. It makes an easy day trip or weekend escape from the capital, and you can combine it with nearby Valparaíso and Viña del Mar.

# Can you swim in it?

San Alfonso del Mar is a **private resort**. The giant lagoon is generally reserved for residents and guests, so if swimming is the goal, **book a stay at the resort** rather than turning up for the day. Check the current access rules before you go, because they can change by season.

# What else to do nearby

- **Isla Negra**: Pablo Neruda's ocean-front house-museum is just down the coast.
- **Valparaíso**: colourful hills, funiculars and some of the best street art in South America.
- **Wine country**: the Casablanca Valley sits between Santiago and the coast. It's perfect for a white-wine stop on the drive.
- **Back in Santiago**: the free, glowing [Bahá'í Temple](/posts/bahai-temple-santiago-chile/) is a good city stop before or after the coast.
- **Algarrobo itself**: a relaxed beach town with seafood restaurants and a yacht club. Just offshore is a small rocky islet that's a protected nesting site for **Humboldt penguins**. You can't land on it, but you can look out at it from the coast.

# When to go

- **Summer (December to February)** is beach season on Chile's central coast, with the warmest weather and the busiest towns. It's also when the lagoon is at its liveliest.
- **Spring and autumn** are quieter and cheaper, but the Pacific stays cold all year and coastal mornings can be grey and misty.
- Outside summer, check what's open at the resort before you book.

# A day trip from Santiago

1. **Morning**: drive west from Santiago and stop in the **Casablanca Valley** for a winery visit.
2. **Midday**: continue to **Algarrobo** for seafood by the sea and a look at San Alfonso del Mar.
3. **Afternoon**: head south along the coast to **Isla Negra** and Neruda's house-museum (check opening days and book ahead).
4. **Evening**: drive back to Santiago, or stay the night on the coast.

# Getting around

Buses run regularly from Santiago to Algarrobo, but **renting a car** gives you the freedom to hop between beach towns, Isla Negra and Valparaíso.

# Frequently asked questions

## Can you visit San Alfonso del Mar for the day?
The lagoon is part of a private resort and is usually reserved for residents and guests. If you want to swim in it, book a stay and check the current access rules first.

## Is the pool filled with seawater?
Yes. Water is pumped from the Pacific Ocean and filtered, which is why it looks like a turquoise lagoon.

## How far is San Alfonso del Mar from Santiago?
About two hours by car, depending on traffic. There are also regular buses from Santiago to Algarrobo.

## Is it still the largest pool in the world?
No. It held the Guinness record from 2006, but bigger lagoons have been built since then.

# Qué más hacer cerca

- **Isla Negra**: la casa-museo de Pablo Neruda frente al mar está a pocos minutos por la costa.
- **Valparaíso**: cerros coloridos, ascensores y uno de los mejores circuitos de arte urbano de Sudamérica.

# Cuándo ir

- **El verano (diciembre a febrero)** es la temporada de playa en la costa central de Chile, con el mejor clima y los pueblos más llenos. También es cuando la laguna tiene más movimiento.
- **Primavera y otoño** son más tranquilos y baratos, pero el Pacífico es frío todo el año y las mañanas en la costa pueden estar nubladas.
- Fuera del verano, revisá qué está abierto en el resort antes de reservar.
